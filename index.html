02 / Original essay

Perspective and the limits of certainty

People rarely encounter the world without interpretation. Memory selects, attention filters, and language gives shape to what can be described. Two people can witness the same event and leave with different accounts—not necessarily because one is dishonest, but because experience is assembled from different points of view.

Recognising this does not mean that every account is equally accurate. It means that careful thinking begins by distinguishing what happened from the meaning we assign to it. Evidence can be tested; assumptions can be questioned; a first impression can be revised.

Awareness, then, is not simply the act of seeing. It is also the willingness to examine the lens through which we see. Intellectual humility is not the rejection of truth, but a method for approaching it with greater care.
